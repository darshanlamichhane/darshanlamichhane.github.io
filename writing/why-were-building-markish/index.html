Why We're Building Markish

By Darshan Lamichhane · Cofounder of Markish · October 16, 2026

We've always been tech people. We like building things, figuring out how systems work, and solving problems with software.

At some point, the three of us started looking for something worth building together. Not necessarily another technical challenge, but an actual problem where the software we built could make someone's work easier.

That's how we came across social media management.

How it started

A conversation with someone working at a marketing agency got us looking into how social media teams actually operate.

From the outside, it seems straightforward. You create content, schedule it, and publish it.

But there's a lot happening between those steps. Planning, managing multiple brands, coordinating with people, reviewing drafts, getting approvals, keeping track of what's going out, and figuring out what's working.

A lot of the work isn't creating the content itself. It's everything around it.

We saw an opportunity there.

Within a week, we had our first MVP of what would become Markish.

It wasn't some perfectly planned product. We had identified a problem, had a rough idea of how we could approach it, and wanted to see whether we could build something useful.

Since then, we've been piloting Markish with marketing agencies, listening to how they work, figuring out what they need, and building around that.

It's been a different experience from simply deciding on a feature and implementing it.

Bigger than agencies

Marketing agencies helped us understand the problem, but we never wanted Markish to be limited to agencies.

Think about someone who's really good at building things but has no idea how to create content or put their work out there. Or a small business owner trying to manage everything themselves. Or an agency juggling multiple clients. Or a large company coordinating content across teams and departments.

Different people, very different scales, but a similar underlying need.

They have something they want to share with the world, and getting it out there shouldn't be unnecessarily complicated.

That's the direction we're taking Markish.

We're building a platform that brings planning, collaboration, scheduling, publishing, and analytics together. Today, Markish supports Instagram, Facebook, and TikTok, and we're continuously working on making those workflows better.

But the bigger idea isn't just putting more features into one product.

We want to make creating, managing, and sharing content easier for anyone who has something worth putting out there.

Whether that's one person working alone or a hundred people working together.

Learning to build more than software

I'm one of three cofounders, and most of what I've done professionally has been technical.

I enjoy engineering. I like understanding how things work, making systems reliable, and figuring out why something breaks.

But building Markish has made me think about things I didn't pay nearly as much attention to before.

You can spend weeks making something technically impressive, but that doesn't automatically mean people need it.

You have to talk to people, understand how they work, make decisions with incomplete information, and sometimes realize that something you thought was important isn't important to the people using it.

We're learning that as we go.

And we're also learning that building a product and building a company are two different challenges.

Neither comes with a straightforward set of instructions.

Still early

We're not pretending to have everything figured out.

There's a lot we want to build, plenty we still need to improve, and a lot more people whose problems we want to understand.

For now, we're building alongside the people using Markish, trying to get the important things right, and seeing where we can take it.

We started with a problem we saw in marketing agencies. But we don't think the problem ends there.

If you're someone trying to put yourself, your work, or your company out into the world, we want Markish to make that easier.

That's what we're building toward.
